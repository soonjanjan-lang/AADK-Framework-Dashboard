    RDD diagrams: what changes when SKUAD starts

    Picture two groups of clients in the same district. One entered supervision a few weeks before the local SKUAD unit started, the other a few weeks after. They are alike in almost every way, much as students who score 49 and 50 in an exam with a pass mark of 50 differ by luck rather than ability. So a sudden jump at the start month is SKUAD's doing. This comparison is called a regression discontinuity design (RDD).

    1. Dots are averages for clients in the same band of months.
    2. Lines show the trend on each side.
    3. The dashed line is the month SKUAD started.
    4. The gap where the lines meet it is SKUAD's effect.

    Where the numbers meet the voices

    The RDD says how much SKUAD changes. Focus group discussions (FGDs) explain why and how, and what gets in the way. The two lines meet to form the framework, which AADK then tests and refines.

    What the focus groups will ask

    Six groups, six to eight people each, analysed by thematic analysis (Braun and Clarke, 2006). These are guiding questions only: no FGD findings exist yet, and none are shown.

    The Community-Based SKUAD Antidadah Empowerment Framework

    Kerangka Pemerkasaan SKUAD Antidadah Berasaskan Komuniti. It combines programme effectiveness indicators, a community engagement mechanism and a community support model, as AADK asked.

    About these numbers

    Every number on this page is synthetic. The records were generated with a fixed random seed, so rebuilding them gives identical values. They reproduce Figure 2 of the proposal exactly.

    Places are codes (KDH-01 to KDH-12 in Kedah, PLS-01 in Perlis); no real person or place is represented.

    Method notes

    • Chart lines are drawn through the dots on each side, each dot weighted by its number of records, exactly as in Figure 2.
    • Formal estimates use rdrobust: local linear fits, a triangular kernel, a data-driven window, robust 95% ranges, and standard errors clustered by locality.
    • The two indices average the standardised parts of each outcome (Kling, Liebman and Katz, 2007).
    • FGDs are analysed by reflexive thematic analysis. They explain the numbers; they do not estimate effects.
